# Example-sentence rules

Examples are the main teaching engine. Every selected vocabulary item and Redemittel card has at least one; every grammar point normally has 2–4.

## A strong example

1. **One clear primary target.** Mark the exact word, expression or grammar form. Mark both meaningful parts of a separable construction.
2. **Natural adult use.** The situation should be plausible outside the textbook plot.
3. **Short enough to repeat.** Prefer one clause; add a subordinate clause only when it teaches the target or a valuable supporting construction.
4. **Shows behavior.** Demonstrate the article/form, collocation, complement, preposition/case, word order, reply or communicative purpose.
5. **Faithful translation.** English should express the same meaning naturally.
6. **Earned reinforcement.** Recycle one supporting lesson word or familiar grammar when it fits naturally. Do not cram the sentence to increase coverage.

Examples are newly authored unless quotation is explicitly necessary. Use roles such as `meine Kollegin` rather than coursebook names.

Good:

- `Unser Betriebsausflug findet dieses Jahr im Mai statt.`
- `Ich wollte mich erkundigen, ob wir etwas mitbringen sollen.`
- `Ich freue mich auf den Betriebsausflug.`

Reject:

- `Die Einladung ist heute wichtig.`
- `Der Betriebsausflug hilft mir heute.`
- `Im Alltag kann ich sich freuen.`
- a dictionary definition presented as a sentence;
- a sentence that depends on remembering the coursebook story;
- an overpacked sentence with several unfamiliar constructions;
- an example whose translation hides incorrect German.

## Forms and targets

For a reflexive verb, agree the pronoun with the subject: `Ich freue mich …`, never `Ich kann sich freuen`. Preserve the verb’s complement in the headword or form note: `sich freuen auf + Akk.`, `sich erkundigen nach + Dat. / ob …`.

Store plain target-language text, English translation and exact target spans. The renderer inserts highlighting. For embedded supporting material, record a separate exact realization internally, but keep the primary highlight visually dominant.

Before accepting a sentence, ask:

- Would an adult plausibly say it?
- Does it teach how the target works?
- Can the learner repeat it without decoding a mini paragraph?
- Does any embedded material improve the sentence rather than merely make it denser?
