# Example-sentence rules

Examples are the main teaching engine. Every selected vocabulary item and Redemittel card has at least one; every grammar point normally has 2–4. The learner hears them, repeats them and meets them again in the games, so a weak example is repeated hundreds of times. Write each one as if it were the only sentence the learner will remember about the target.

## Three tests for every example

1. **Would an adult say or write it in real life?** At work, at home, with friends, at an office, in a shop, in an e-mail, in the speaking exam.
2. **Does it show how the target behaves?** Its article and form, its preposition and case, its word order, its register, what it is used for.
3. **Is it worth remembering?** Concrete enough to picture, short enough to repeat in one breath.

## The quality bar

- **Specific, not generic.** A small concrete detail (a time, a place, a person, a reason, a feeling) turns a correct sentence into a memorable one. *Die Vorlesung beginnt um acht.* is correct but forgettable; *Die Vorlesung beginnt um acht, deshalb nehme ich den ersten Bus.* gives the learner a scene.
- **Rich enough for the level.** At B1 many examples carry one connector or second clause the course has taught (*weil, dass, wenn, obwohl, damit, deshalb, trotzdem*). A1-flat sentences such as *X ist schön* or *Ich habe ein X* teach nothing beyond the word.
- **One clear target.** Add at most one supporting structure. Never pack several new words or constructions into one line.
- **Useful.** The learner could change two words and use the sentence about their own life, in an e-mail or in the exam.
- **Not silly.** No absurd combinations, forced jokes, childish scenes, random facts, or sentences that only make sense inside the course book's plot.
- **Not empty.** No *Das ist wichtig*, *Das ist interessant*, *Das ist ein X* as the whole sentence unless that structure is the target.
- **Varied.** Mix statements, questions and requests, *ich, du, wir* and third persons, present and Perfekt. No more than about 40 % of a sheet's examples start with *Ich*.
- **Short.** Usually 7–14 words. A Redemittel example may be a little longer when the frame needs its situation.
- **Faithful translation.** Natural English with exactly the same meaning.
- **Original.** Newly written. Never copy the source's sentences, character names, places, plot or statistics; use roles (*meine Kollegin, unser Vermieter*) or neutral first names that are not the source's.

## Upgrades

| Weak | Why | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| *Die Einladung ist heute wichtig.* | filler, says nothing | *Danke für die Einladung! Soll ich einen Salat mitbringen?* |
| *Das Seminar ist interessant.* | empty adjective | *Im Seminar diskutieren wir jede Woche einen Zeitungsartikel.* |
| *Ich habe einen Termin.* | A1-flat | *Ich muss den Termin beim Zahnarzt leider verschieben.* |
| *Er ist stolz.* | no complement | *Meine Eltern sind stolz darauf, dass ich jetzt auf Deutsch telefonieren kann.* |
| *Wir feiern.* | nothing to picture | *Am Freitag feiern wir den Abschied von unserer Kollegin.* |
| *Der Hund trinkt im Büro Kaffee.* | silly | *Unser Hund darf mit ins Büro, wenn er ruhig bleibt.* |
| *Herr Kaminski war so stolz auf seinen Garten.* | the book's character and plot | *Ich bin stolz auf meine Fortschritte in Deutsch.* |
| *Im Alltag kann ich sich freuen.* | wrong German | *Ich freue mich schon auf das Wochenende.* |

## Forms and targets

1. **One clear primary target.** Mark the exact word, expression or grammar form. Mark both meaningful parts of a separable construction (*stelle … vor*).
2. **Show the behavior the card teaches.** For a reflexive verb, agree the pronoun with the subject: *Ich freue mich …*, never *Ich kann sich freuen*. Keep the complement in the form note: *sich freuen auf + Akk.*, *sich erkundigen nach + Dat. / ob …*. A Perfekt example is often the most useful one for a verb.
3. **Recycle naturally.** An example may reuse one supporting lesson word or an earlier grammar point when it fits. That is the preferred way to raise coverage without adding cards.

Store plain target-language text, English translation and exact target spans. The renderer inserts the highlighting. For embedded supporting material, record a separate exact realization internally, and keep the primary highlight visually dominant.

## Before accepting a sentence

- Would an adult plausibly say it?
- Does it teach how the target works?
- Can the learner picture it and repeat it in one breath?
- Is it the learner's own, not the source's?
- Does any embedded material improve the sentence rather than merely make it denser?
