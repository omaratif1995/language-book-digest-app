# Learner-sheet specification

The HTML is a compact reference to study, not a workbook or a record of the build process.

A small header may identify the book, level, lesson and topic. After it, render exactly three `h2` sections in this order:

1. `Wortschatz`
2. `Grammatik`
3. `Redemittel`

No other learner-facing section or meta panel is allowed. Do not add a goal, Start route, summary, article legend, navigation, source status, media warning, practice, quiz, culture box, progress indicator, strategy, inventory or coverage note.

If embedded audio was requested, a compact whole-lesson play/pause control may appear inside the identifying header and small pronunciation buttons may sit beside the German strings they play. These are controls, not a fourth section. Do not add an audio section, transcript panel, playlist, speed selector or collapsible player.

## Wortschatz

Use a few meaningful semantic groups when they improve scanning. Do not create Core/Later tiers.

Every selected item is an individual card with:

- clean headword and concise English meaning;
- article and useful plural/no-plural information for a noun;
- useful complement, reflexive/separable form, preposition/case or collocation for a verb when relevant; and
- one marked, translated, natural example.

A second example is justified only by a different important source-taught use. A complete small explicit word field should stay coherent. Incidental text words do not become cards simply because they occurred.

Forms are supporting text, not an excuse for a mini dictionary entry. Omit unhelpful principal-part clutter when the lesson does not require it.

## Grammatik

Use one rule card per concept the learner needs to distinguish. Include all explicitly taught grammar, including small rules. Separate concepts when merging would make the learner decode too much at once.

Each card contains:

- `meaning`: what the structure communicates or when it is used;
- `rule`: the shortest accurate formation/choice explanation;
- one or more compact `patterns`;
- normally 2–4 marked, translated examples; and
- optional `contrast` only when it prevents a likely error.

Let examples carry explanation. Do not copy a complete textbook table when a compact pattern plus examples teaches the same decision. Do not impose a number of rule cards.

## Redemittel

Group by communicative function supported by the lesson. All reusable speaking, writing and email language stays in this one section; do not split it into skill-specific sections.

Each phrase card contains:

- reusable `frame` first, with placeholders such as `…`;
- short English meaning;
- one concise use cue;
- register only when it matters; and
- one marked, translated example.

Combine truly interchangeable variants when this saves repetition and the difference remains clear. Keep opposite moves, different constructions or materially different registers separate. Never copy a whole letter, dialogue or reading passage.

## Density and presentation

Prefer the visual density of the earlier v3 sheets: short cards, modest group headings and generous enough spacing to scan. Keep article colours on the noun itself if useful, but no legend. Keep all selected learning content visible; do not use folding, tabs or hidden “more” content.

The page should feel finished without a footer, recap or instruction block. Its three sections are the study experience.
